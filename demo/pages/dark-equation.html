Simple harmonic motion

A mass on a spring, pulled away from rest and released, moves back and forth in a pattern that repeats forever in an ideal system. Its position is a cosine wave, and a single number, the angular frequency, sets how fast it cycles.

The period depends only on k and m

Hooke's law gives a restoring force F = −kx that always points back toward equilibrium. Substituting it into Newton's second law, m d²x/dt² = −kx, gives a sinusoid whose frequency is set by the stiffness and the inertia alone. The starting amplitude A changes how far the mass travels, but not how often it completes a cycle.
